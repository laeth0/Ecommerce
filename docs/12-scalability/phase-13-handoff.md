Documentation, registered users, a warm cache hit, ready replica, one fast route or completed dump cannot satisfy these gates.

## Explain before proceeding

1. Derive 300 global/150 single-source full-mix policy ceilings and their distinction from supported throughput.
2. Explain 1,000 sessions, three verified source groups and normal refresh/setup/cleanup economics.
3. Explain why the paced user tier is not 1,000 simultaneous requests and why closed-loop load can hide saturation.
4. Identify the limiting resource using plans, locks, pools, CPU/WAL/disk, queue/service or provider amplification.
5. Explain primary publication/price/authority guard and why a cache may fail its own read-reduction gate.
6. Explain stale-cache fill/restore namespace, standby fence before fresh snapshot and cluster-wide replicated-data boundary.
7. Explain global UUID uniqueness versus partition key, hash-line pruning and why partitioning does not parallelize a hot stock row.
8. Compute event multiplicity/relay ceiling and net backlog drain, including retries/parking/fairness.
9. Explain safe compatible rollback after new writes and why copied data cannot invent accepted history.
10. Distinguish demonstrated capacity, unreached target and 10k/100k analytical assumptions.

## Inputs for production infrastructure

Carry exact artifact/schema/data/policy/resource/topology fingerprints, measured per-route service/latency/failure/CPU/memory/connection budgets, worker/provider/queue amplification, debt/scan/disk horizon, source/session/authority requirements and safe RPO/RTO.

The baseline stays PostgreSQL-first unless a specific approved mechanism has actual activation evidence. No further service extraction, cache/routing/partition rollout or cloud spend is authorized by an analytical capacity forecast.
